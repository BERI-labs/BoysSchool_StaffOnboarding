## 1. Welcome to Habs Boys

Haberdashers' Boys' School ("Habs Boys") traces its history back to 1689, when Robert Aske, a member of the Haberdashers' Guild, left a sum of money to found a school in Hoxton, East London, to educate 20 poor boys. Following redevelopment of the Hoxton site, the school reopened in June 1875 and was divided into two schools, educating boys and girls separately. The Boys' School moved to Hampstead in 1898 and then to Elstree in 1961, becoming Haberdashers' Boys' School, Elstree.

The school's mission is "to provide the very best education for the boys in our care," aiming to be "a modern, forward-thinking community based on traditional foundations." Its purpose is to ensure that every student is empowered to make a profound impact in the world — whether by driving positive change in society or simply being a good person who makes a difference to those around them.

The Staff Handbook is intended to provide general information and guidance; it is not a legal or binding contract and should be read alongside an individual's Contract of Employment, the Employment Manual and associated policies. The current version is kept up to date on SharePoint. Questions about the handbook should go to the Deputy Head Operations (teaching staff) or HR (professional services staff).

**Source:** Staff Handbook, "Welcome from the Headmaster" (front matter).

## 11. Attendance and Absence — Teaching Staff

All teaching staff are expected to be in school throughout the school day and to work any additional hours necessary for the proper performance of their duties. Staff who are free during period 5, with no cover rota period or other commitment, may leave the site at 3.30pm, having informed their line manager beforehand. Staff are also expected to be in school for Open Day, all professional development and preparation days, and entrance examination days, even if these fall outside their usual working days. Absences must be logged on MyHabs in line with current HR policy.

**Senior School — planned absence:** Planned absences (CPD, examining, trips, medical appointments) require permission via the Absence & Cover Request Form, found in the Absence and Cover area of the staff dashboard. The form is needed whenever you require cover, are unavailable to cover a lesson, or will be off-site/off-timetable before 4pm. You must speak to your Head of Department before submitting the form, and the request is not authorised until confirmed by email from the Deputy Head Operations — do not make arrangements until approval is confirmed. Cover should be arranged within your department where possible. CPD must not be booked before consent is sought. A minimum of 48 hours' notice is required; if this isn't possible, email the Deputy Head Operations and their PA directly as well as completing the form. It is essential that your timetable is kept correct on iSAMS so cover can be arranged — report any changes to help@habselstree.org.uk and sstack@habselstree.org.uk.

**Prep and Pre-Prep — unplanned absence:** Contact the Head of Prep and Pre-Prep by 7am on the first day of absence and each subsequent day.

**Prep and Pre-Prep — planned absence:** For CPD, seek approval from your line manager, then the Head of Prep and Pre-Prep and DHT Academic, before booking the course. For a whole day or longer that isn't CPD, request permission from the Head of Prep and Pre-Prep, copying the DHT Academic. Further detail is in the Absence and Cover Management Protocol on Teams.

**Source:** Staff Handbook, "Attendance and Absence" section (pp.7–8, 21).

## 14. Cover

Every teacher is required to take cover periods.

**Senior School:** Staff should expect at least one cover period a week (more if under-loaded). Cover is distributed as fairly as possible, but staff may occasionally be asked to cover outside the rota during periods of high absence. New staff are not asked to cover in their first term and join the rota from their second term. Timetables show "cover rota" periods — these should be kept clear or flexible, as they guarantee availability if cover is needed; using a cover rota period for a meeting requires an Absence Request Form. Specialist subject cover is assigned wherever possible. The PA to the Deputy Head Operations sends cover details via an iSAMS-generated email by 8am each morning, though emergency cover can be requested through the day — check email before period 1 and throughout the day if timetabled to cover. Cover work is set by the absent colleague or, if the absence is unplanned, by the Head of Department; the covering teacher should also be briefed on any students with an Individual Needs Plan. Cover work should be done silently — no videos or films. Sixth Form classes are not normally covered, except for tests or at a Head of Department's discretion.

**Prep and Pre-Prep:** Cover is allocated by the Cover Manager (usually the Deputy Head Academic). Cover work is set by the absent colleague or, for unplanned absence, the Phase Leader, with the same expectation of briefing on Individual Needs Plans. Planned cover work is saved in the Cover Deposit folder on Teams.

**Source:** Staff Handbook, "Cover" section (p.9).

## 16. Fire Evacuation and Emergency Procedures

When a fire alarm sounds, all buildings must be evacuated immediately via the nearest exit to the assembly point shown in the Fire Evacuation Assembly plan on SharePoint. Main assembly areas are the Senior School AstroTurf, the Prep School playground and the Pre-Prep playground. Teachers should accompany their classes to the muster point, keeping students walking briskly and in silence — maintaining discipline during an evacuation is a collective staff responsibility.

**Senior School:** Once on the AstroTurf, staff should listen for instructions from the Senior Leadership Team (in hi-vis jackets). Registers are not taken at the muster point — a search of affected buildings is used instead.

**Prep School:** Students gather on the Prep playground; staff check all students are present and report this to the Prep Leadership Team, who coordinate by radio with the Senior School AstroTurf.

**Pre-Prep School:** Students gather in the Pre-Prep playground; Class Teachers check all students are present and report to the Prep Leadership Team, then wait for further instructions.

More broadly, the Fire Evacuation Procedure and Missing Child email procedures are published on SharePoint and should be reviewed as soon as you join.

**Source:** Staff Handbook, "Fire Evacuation" section (p.11) and "Emergency Procedures" section (p.24).

## 18. Induction

The Director of Initial Teacher Training oversees the induction programme (ext 1739). In the Prep and Pre-Prep this is supplemented by the Head of Prep and Pre-Prep. New staff complete a day of induction before the start of the academic year, followed by weekly Monday lunchtime meetings (1pm, Olympian Suite) for the first two terms — these should be prioritised over other meetings. Each new colleague is assigned a subject mentor as their first point of contact for day-to-day queries. All new colleagues are observed by their Head of Department/line manager and a member of SLT in their first term, with employment confirmed after a three-month probationary period.

Habs Boys is the North London Hub of the National Modern Languages SCITT and also runs its own in-house Graduate Teacher Training Programme; trainees are employed by the Habs Schools and follow the Teacher Apprenticeship route with the University of Buckingham towards QTS and a PGCE, supported by an in-house training programme aligned with the ECF, run fortnightly in the Innovation Space at the Girls' School.

Several Early Career Teachers (ECTs) join each year across the Senior, Prep and Pre-Prep Schools and complete their two-year statutory induction, following the Early Career Framework in partnership with the Girls' School. Habs is a Regional Centre for the Independent Schools Induction Panel (IStip), the ECT awarding body, and hosts three conferences a year attended by ECTs from other local independent schools.

**Source:** Staff Handbook, "Induction" section (p.14).

## 20. Progress Grades

Progress grades give a snapshot of a student's effort and attainment over roughly half a term. The approach-to-learning grade is a professional judgement by the teacher; the attainment grade reflects the standard a student is currently working at and performing to — it is not a prediction of an external examination outcome. Grades go to students and parents, and Form Tutors discuss them with tutees, setting SMART targets to support progress. In the Prep and Pre-Prep, students in Year 1 and above receive progress grades at least termly.

**Source:** Staff Handbook, "Progress Grades" section (p.16).

## 27. Visiting Speakers

The Visiting Speaker Policy (VSP) is a statutory Prevent-strategy requirement. Anyone inviting a speaker into school must complete two forms (both appendices to the policy): the first — checking the speaker — must be approved by a member of SLT before the speaker is confirmed; the second is completed by the speaker themselves, ideally before arriving on site, and is effectively a commitment not to express extremist views. If a student has organised the speaker, a member of staff must complete the forms on their behalf. Both signed forms must reach the PA to the Deputy Head Academic before the speaker addresses students — as much notice as possible should be given (the day before is not acceptable except in genuine emergencies, such as a late replacement speaker).

**Source:** Staff Handbook, "Visiting Speakers" section (p.20).
